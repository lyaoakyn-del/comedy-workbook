# Joke Structure

## The Basic Unit: Setup + Punchline

Every joke has two parts:

**Setup** — creates an expectation. Establishes context, makes a premise, sets up what the audience thinks is coming.

**Punchline** — breaks the expectation. The surprise, the twist, the absurd conclusion the audience didn't see coming.

The formula isn't complicated. The execution is.

---

## Setup Rules

**Keep it short.** Every word in the setup is a word before the laugh. Cut anything that doesn't directly set up the punchline.

**Be specific.** "My mom" is generic. "My mom, who still calls Instagram 'the Facebook for photos'" is specific. Specificity creates recognition and makes the premise feel real.

**One idea per setup.** If you're explaining two things, you've lost them before the punchline.

**Don't signal the punchline.** If the setup implies where you're going, the punchline won't land. The audience should be surprised but in hindsight think "of course."

---

## Punchline Rules

**Funny word last.** The last word of the sentence carries the most weight. Put the funniest, most surprising word there.

❌ "I was so tired that I fell asleep on the subway platform, which is embarrassing."
✅ "I fell asleep on the subway platform. Which I'm told is a symptom of being embarrassing, but also scurvy."

**Shorter is almost always better.** If you can cut a word and the joke still works, cut it.

**Don't explain.** If you feel the need to explain why it's funny, rewrite the punchline. A punchline that requires explanation isn't a punchline.

---

## The Tag

A tag is a second (or third) punchline that follows the main one. The audience is already laughing — you hit them again.

Tags work when the main punchline opens up a new direction. Not every joke needs one.

---

## Callbacks

A callback is when you reference an earlier joke later in the set. Used well, callbacks get bigger laughs than the original joke because the audience feels rewarded for remembering.

Plan callbacks when building set lists. Mark potential callbacks in your joke files with `#колбэк`.

---

## Common Problems

**Setup too long** — audience loses the thread before you get to the punchline. Test: can you cut the first sentence entirely?

**Punchline too predictable** — audience finishes your sentence in their head. Fix: find a more unexpected angle on the same premise.

**Punchline buried** — the funny word isn't last. Restructure the sentence.

**Over-explaining** — adding "...you know?" or "...which is crazy" after the punchline. Cut it. If it's not funny on its own, rework it.

**Generic premise** — "isn't it weird how..." observations that everyone has had. Find your specific take, not the universal one.

---

## The 3-Version Rule

When developing a punchline: write at least 3 versions before choosing. The first version is almost never the best one. The second is usually better. The third often surprises you.

This is where the AI buddy helps — ask it for 3 alternative punchlines on the same premise.
